# How to Generate Prompts for Other Brands

This guide shows you how to adapt the workflow for **any D2C brand** in any category.

---

## Quick Start: 3 Steps

### Step 1: Fill Out the Brand Brief Template
Use the same template as Nykaa—just with your brand's info.

### Step 2: Use the Web Tool
Open `index.html` in browser → fill form → workflow generates everything

### Step 3: Get Your Prompts
Copy prompts → paste into DALL-E 3, Midjourney, or Adobe Firefly

## Step-by-Step: Complete Example with Different Brands

### **Example 1: Fashion Brand**

#### Input Template (Brand Brief)

```
PRODUCT & BRAND DETAILS

Brand Name: UNBOUND (Indian sustainable fashion)
Product Name: Organic Cotton T-Shirt (Oversized)

PRODUCT
- Core features/USP:
  • 100% organic cotton (GOTS certified)
  • Oversized fit, unisex wearable
  • Zero-waste dyeing process
  • Lasts 100+ washes

- What problem does it solve?
  Fast fashion causes waste. Consumers want quality basics that last.

- Price Positioning: Mid-premium (₹1,800–2,200)


TARGET AUDIENCE
- Primary demographic: 
  Male & female, 22–35, urban India (Delhi, Bangalore, Mumbai)
  College grads, conscious about sustainability
  
- What do they value most?
  Quality + ethics + timeless design
  They want to feel good about their purchase
  
- Where do they discover products?
  Instagram influencers (sustainable fashion creators)
  Amazon & brand website
  Sustainable fashion communities


BRAND PERSONALITY & VISUAL IDENTITY
- 3 words: Conscious, Timeless, Authentic
- Brand values: Sustainability, minimalism, durability
- Color palette: Off-white, charcoal gray, sage green, natural linen


MARKET CONTEXT
- Main competitors (5):
  1. Nicobar (Indian sustainable fashion)
  2. Mango (premium ethical basics)
  3. CottonWorld (mass-market basics)
  4. The Minimal Co (indie sustainable)
  5. Warp + Weft (direct-to-consumer)

- Your unique angle vs. them:
  Nicobar = more design-focused
  Mango = more luxury, international
  CottonWorld = mass market (we're premium)
  The Minimal Co = similar but we have better production scale
  → Our angle: "Sustainable luxury for everyday"

- What you WON'T communicate:
  Eco-guilt ("save the planet or die")
  Greenwashing (no fake sustainability claims)
  Overly cool factor (not streetwear—just quality basic)


SUCCESS CRITERIA
- What feeling should this image evoke?
  "I can wear this forever—and feel good about it"
  Quality without arrogance
  Timeless, not trendy

- Where will this image be used?
  E-commerce product page
  Instagram feed, Stories, Reels
  Paid ads (Pinterest, Instagram)

```

#### Expected Output: 3 Concepts

**Concept 1: ORIGIN STORY** (Heritage angle)
- Focus on: Organic cotton field, production process close-up, GOTS certification
- Visual: Macro of fabric texture, hands holding raw cotton, minimal composition
- Vibe: "See where this starts—purity"

**Concept 2: EVERYDAY LUXURY** (Lifestyle angle)
- Focus on: Person wearing the t-shirt in morning routine, calm setting
- Visual: Minimalist bedroom, natural light, person looks calm and centered
- Vibe: "This is what timeless looks like"

**Concept 3: DURABILITY PROOF** (Function angle)
- Focus on: Product detail, stitching, hang tag, fabric weave macro
- Visual: Close-up of stitching quality, label showing care instructions, texture
- Vibe: "Built to last"

## How to Use the Web Tool for Your Brand

### Option A: Use Web Tool (Easiest)

1. **Open the tool:**
   - Deploy `index.html` to Vercel, OR
   - Open `index.html` locally in browser

2. **Fill out the form with YOUR brand data:**
   - Brand name, product name
   - Features (3–5 bullet points)
   - Target audience (age, income, values, where they discover products)
   - Brand personality (3 words)
   - Colors (3–5 color names)
   - Use case, tone, what to avoid
   - Competitors (list 3–5)

3. **Click "Analyze Competitors"**
   - AI identifies competitor visual patterns
   - Review: does this competitor list make sense?

4. **Click "Generate Concepts"**
   - AI creates 3 strategic image concepts
   - Review: do these match your brand positioning?

5. **Click "Generate Prompts"**
   - AI converts concepts to image generation prompts
   - Copy each prompt

6. **Paste into Image Generator:**
   - Open DALL-E 3, Midjourney, or Adobe Firefly
   - Paste the prompt
   - Generate images
   - Review + iterate if needed

---

### Option B: Use Template Manually (If Tool is Down)

1. **Fill out the Brand Brief template** (like examples above)
2. **For Competitor Analysis:**
   - List 5 competitors
   - Note their visual style, positioning, weakness
   - Write down "use signals" (working trends) and "avoid signals" (clichés)
3. **Design 3 Concepts:**
   - Concept 1: Heritage/Origin story
   - Concept 2: Lifestyle/Aspirational use
   - Concept 3: Function/Proof of benefit
4. **Write Image Prompts:**
   - Main prompt (500+ words describing the exact scene)
   - Negative prompt (what NOT to show)
   - Specifications (aspect ratio, best use)
5. **Generate images**

## Best Practices by Category

### **Skincare/Beauty**
- **Concept 1:** Close-up of ingredient or before/after proof
- **Concept 2:** Person with visibly glowing/healthy skin in natural setting
- **Concept 3:** Product detail, luxury packaging, texture macro
- **Color Palette:** Warm golds/rose golds, natural skin tones, jewel tones
- **Avoid:** Overly retouched skin, heavy makeup, clinical vibes

### **Fashion/Apparel**
- **Concept 1:** Fabric detail, material sourcing, craftsmanship
- **Concept 2:** Person wearing item in everyday context (work, coffee, commute)
- **Concept 3:** Product flat lay with lifestyle props (shoes with socks, jacket with scarf)
- **Color Palette:** Natural fibers (beige, cream), heritage colors (indigo, rust)
- **Avoid:** Overly styled, runway aesthetic, disconnected from real life

### **Food/Supplements**
- **Concept 1:** Ingredient source (farm, harvesting, plant)
- **Concept 2:** Meal/snack in social setting (breakfast table, lunch break)
- **Concept 3:** Product close-up, nutrition facts visible, freshness emphasized
- **Color Palette:** Warm, food-appealing (golden, earth tones, greens)
- **Avoid:** Clinical/medicinal look, artificial colors, overly staged food

### **Tech/Electronics**
- **Concept 1:** Technology visible (closeup of sensor, circuit, interface)
- **Concept 2:** Product in use during normal activity (working, traveling, creating)
- **Concept 3:** Product detail, materials/sustainability, design precision
- **Color Palette:** Neutral/industrial (grays, whites) + 1–2 accent colors
- **Avoid:** Overly futuristic, generic tech aesthetics, sterile white backgrounds

### **Home/Lifestyle**
- **Concept 1:** Material/craftsmanship detail, texture macro
- **Concept 2:** Product in actual home setting, in use by real person
- **Concept 3:** Lifestyle composition (object styled with complementary items)
- **Color Palette:** Warm, inviting (earth tones, pastels, natural)
- **Avoid:** Magazine-overly-styled, too minimal, disconnected from real homes

## Common Questions

### **Q: Can I use this for luxury brands?**
**A:** Yes! Just adjust the input:
- Highlight exclusivity, craftsmanship, heritage
- Use luxury competitor references (Hermès, Aesop, etc.)
- Emphasize premium materials in concepts
- Use jewel tones and metallic accents

Example: "Premium Indian leather goods inspired by Hermès"

### **Q: What if my brand is B2B, not D2C?**
**A:** Adapt the framework:
- Instead of consumer lifestyle, show business use case
- Concept 2 becomes: "Product in professional setting (office, factory, meeting)"
- Emphasize efficiency, ROI, problem-solving
- Use business-appropriate color palettes (corporate blues, grays)

### **Q: How do I iterate if the output misses my brand?**
**A:** 
1. Revise the input brief (be more specific about tone/colors)
2. Re-run the workflow from Step 2
3. If generated images miss, edit the prompt:
   - Add specific color names (not "nice colors")
   - Add specific lighting (not "make it look good")
   - Add cultural/heritage details
   - Run again

### **Q: Can I combine concepts?**
**A:** Yes! You can:
- Mix Concept 1 (heritage) + Concept 2 (lifestyle) into one image
- Run 6 variations: 3 concepts × 2 variations each
- A/B test which performs best
- Use winner as hero image, others in secondary carousel

### **Q: What if competitors are international, not local?**
**A:** 
- List both local + international competitors
- The workflow handles either
- Just note any geographic differences (e.g., "Adidas is global; we're India-focused")
